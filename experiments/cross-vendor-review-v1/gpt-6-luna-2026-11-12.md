## 2. What earlier reviews missed, and where I disagree

The Fable review’s central criticism—that the identity thesis has no support on real memory and general frames are source-bound rather than paraphrase-invariant—is well supported by the included evidence. I agree with that diagnosis. I would qualify its specific “3.2% ceiling” as a ceiling on the particular recorded traffic and signature method, not a general upper bound on possible identity value. The report itself says the estimate is loose and mostly concerns restatements already handled by surface-level kv supersession. `experiments/discourse-unit-identity-v1/RESULTS.md`

The bundle also contains a sharper design flaw than the prior reviews emphasize: source-bound validation can certify **different propositions that reuse the same source words**, because it checks word occurrence and coverage, not their semantic roles or composition. For example, in “Omar may not approve invoices,” a candidate could put `may` in a source-bound filler while encoding `approve` as negated; all words could be covered, yet the modal scope could be wrong. That follows from the documented checks and the implementation, not from a measured exploit. `decisions/0024-general-source-bound-frames.md`; `packages/core/src/source-bound.ts`; `packages/core/src/agent-native.ts`

The material repeatedly treats literal retention as a semantic safety floor, but it is only a *presence* check. It cannot establish that a retained number, date, name, or relative time is attached to the right role, or that a condition’s polarity and scope are preserved. The bundle admits this for strict versus inclusive bounds; the same limitation applies more broadly. `decisions/0024-general-source-bound-frames.md`; `experiments/inclusive-bound-v1/RESULTS.md`; `packages/core/src/literal-retention.ts`

A further untested assumption: broad word coverage is a useful proxy for meaning preservation. The source-bound gate can reject omitted content words, but neither it nor literal retention tests entailment, argument assignment, scope, tense, or whether the selected frame matches the sentence. The Fable review notes frame-choice limits, but the underlying issue is broader: the core’s checks certify a parse’s *shape and anchoring*, while the agent chooses the interpretation. `decisions/0024-general-source-bound-frames.md`; `packages/core/src/source-bound.ts`; `packages/core/src/agent-native.ts`

## 3. Is the core idea worth continuing?

**Narrow it, while keeping semantic identity as a research track.** The universal claim—same meaning yields the same identity across paraphrases and languages—has no support in this bundle. On real work-log units, identity yield is zero; on repeated extraction of the same sentence, agreement is 7/12; and the English general frames explicitly allow “fast” and “quick” to have distinct identities. Cross-language convergence is 0/5. `experiments/discourse-unit-identity-v1/RESULTS.md`; `reports/independent-evaluation/2026-10-07-round2-v17-live/REPORT.md`; `decisions/0024-general-source-bound-frames.md`; `README.md`

That does not make the whole project worthless. Deterministic schema checks, literal-retention checks, refusal, provenance, and exact recall can be useful without claiming universal semantic identity. The project’s own scope amendment already describes this narrower role. But records passing these gates should be presented as **validated source-bound parses**, not trusted semantic identities, unless and until held-out tests demonstrate fidelity and convergence. `VISION.md`; `decisions/0026-working-scope-checkable-memory.md`; `experiments/source-locator-v1/RESULTS.md`

## 4. Design review: `fingerprint.ts`, `agent-native.ts`, `literal-retention.ts`, `source-bound.ts`

**Identity instability and false splits.** `semanticIdentityProjection` includes `kind`, term `type`, and the distinction between `id` and `value`; the v17 report gives concrete same-source splits on kind and term type. The projection also preserves raw open-term wording apart from `basicIdentifier` normalization on selected identifier fields. So paraphrases, translations, and representational choices can produce distinct identities even when the asserted meaning is the same. `packages/core/src/fingerprint.ts`; `reports/independent-evaluation/2026-10-07-round2-v17-live/REPORT.md`

**Identity for candidates that change meaning.** The gates do not prove semantic correctness. For general frames, every filler can be anchored in the source while being assigned to the wrong role; source-word coverage still passes. For legacy frames, `source-bound.ts` deliberately skips uncovered-word checking whenever any clause is non-general; it only checks fillers occur in the source. The numeric/date/literal gate checks presence somewhere in semantic fields, not correct attachment or interpretation. Hence an identity can be issued for a candidate that changes meaning while retaining all source words and literals. `packages/core/src/source-bound.ts`; `packages/core/src/literal-retention.ts`; `packages/core/src/agent-native.ts`

**Potential normalization hazard.** `fingerprint.ts` normalizes strings in `type`, `id`, `ref`, `unit`, and `format` through `basicIdentifier`, but leaves `value` recursively represented. This creates field-dependent identity behavior: the same text can normalize differently depending on whether an extractor chooses `id` or `value`, precisely one of the representation choices implicated in the v17 splits. `packages/core/src/fingerprint.ts`; `reports/independent-evaluation/2026-10-07-round2-v17-live/REPORT.md`

**Literal-retention gaps.** The check compares sets of numeric values and identifiers, so it does not preserve multiplicity: a source saying “retry twice, then retry twice” can be matched by one candidate occurrence of `2`. It also treats literal presence separately from semantic attachment. Number words are a finite list, and the displayed table lacks English 1 and 21–29 entries (while compounds may be assembled from tens and units); ordinals are explicitly excluded. These are policy limits that could allow quantities to be dropped without triggering the gate. Relative-time detection is also pattern-based: some English expressions (“by Friday”) are effectively caught because `Friday` is a weekday, while less common temporal phrases may not be. `packages/core/src/literal-retention.ts`

**Source-bound coverage gaps.** `source-bound.ts` marks a source token covered if it appears in *any* filler, and marks all matching occurrences covered. Reusing one filler can therefore cover repeated source words without representing their separate roles. It also permits arbitrary uncovered digits on the assumption literal retention owns them, and it uses a small fixed English closed-word list. More importantly, mixed legacy/general candidates skip the content-word coverage check entirely. These create avenues for identities on incomplete or mis-scoped candidates. `packages/core/src/source-bound.ts`; `decisions/0024-general-source-bound-frames.md`

## 5. Next three experiments and three stops

1. **Blind semantic adversarial audit of issued candidates.** Freeze 200 fresh English sources, candidate outputs, and a rubric before extraction; have two reviewers blind-score role assignment, polarity, modality, conditions, dates, and paraphrase. **Pass:** at least 95% faithful among issued records, with zero critical polarity/threshold/date losses; **fail:** below 90% or any critical loss. 90–95% is inconclusive and requires a larger sample.
2. **Repeatability and representation-equivalence test.** Run two independent extractions per source on 100 held-out sentences, then canonicalize only source-underdetermined labels in a separately versioned candidate projection. **Pass:** at least 95/100 same-source identity agreement and at least 90% agreement on manually adjudicated equivalent candidates; **fail:** below 80/100 same-source agreement or any increase in false merges beyond 1%. Otherwise revise and repeat.
3. **Real-traffic narrow-use trial.** On a fresh, shareable sample of real agent traffic, test a pre-specified narrow set (e.g. explicit status key-value facts and exact identifiers), comparing recall and task answers against the existing discourse view. **Pass:** nonzero identity coverage and at least 2% token reduction with no exact-literal or answer-recall regression; **fail:** below 0.5% useful coverage or any safety/literal regression.

**Stop:**

1. Stop calling general-frame output a semantic identity or implying it is paraphrase- or language-invariant; call it a source-bound candidate/parse until judged evidence supports more.
2. Stop adding predicates, frames, or prompt refinements from author-written probes. Keep the frozen contract until a held-out baseline and changed-version comparison are both run.
3. Stop treating literal retention and word coverage as meaning verification; do not use identity alone as permission to deduplicate or compact records.

## Verdict

Continue OpenLunum as a narrowed, checkable source-record and retrieval project; suspend the universal semantic-identity claim. The strongest evidence supports exact retrieval and deterministic refusal scaffolding, while identity fails on real units, is unstable across repeated extraction, and is not multilingual. Keep measuring semantic identity only in versioned, held-out studies, and label current fingerprints as hashes of validated structures rather than proof of shared meaning.
